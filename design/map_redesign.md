# The level map: three big swings (for Steven to pick from)

Today's map is a winding road of round numbered pins through themed scenery, which reads as "candy
saga". Each swing below keeps the feeling of a trip, makes zones (now opened with stars at toll gates)
feel like places, and gives detours somewhere natural to live. None is built yet.

## 1. The glovebox road atlas

The map is a folded paper road atlas lying open on the passenger seat. Each zone is a page: its own
printed map style, a page number, a legend in the corner, a coffee ring. Levels are delivery addresses
pinned along a route drawn in marker, with your earlier routes as pen lines that are slightly wrong and
crossed out. Detours are highlighter loops off the main route with a sticky note ("Mr. Frosty, ice cream,
go before 3"). Toll gates are page edges: to get to the next page you need enough stars, and the page
corner is folded down until then. Turning a page is the zone transition (a big paper flip, the new page's
title in a typeface of its own). The Baron's work is printed on the map in red: "Hoardly Inc. property",
closed roads, his toll booths.

- **Why it fits:** a courier lives in an atlas; it's ours and nobody else's in the genre; pages make
  zones discrete by construction.
- **What it costs:** a new art direction for the map screen (paper textures, marker strokes, a page-flip
  transition), and every zone needs a distinct print style.
- **Risk:** paper can feel static; the van needs to drive on it (a toy van rolling over the page).

## 2. The dispatch board

The map is the dispatch office wall: a big cork board and a city map behind it. Each level is a job
ticket (a little card with the address, the number of packages, a star stamp once done) pinned to where
it is in town. Red string connects the jobs in order: it's the route you've driven. Zones are districts
outlined in coloured tape, with the district's name on a label-maker strip. A locked district has a
"HOARDLY INC. — NO ENTRY" notice stapled over it, which tears off when you have the stars. Detours are
odd jobs on yellow sticky notes pinned off to the side, with a doodle of the prize. The radio (Dispatch,
from the agreed plan) sits on the shelf and talks when you come back.

- **Why it fits:** it's the business we're running, and it's the Baron's story told on our wall (his
  notices, his trucks drawn in, newspaper clippings as you beat bosses). Tickets show more than a number:
  title, packages, a hint of the twist.
- **What it costs:** a flat, mostly static screen (cheap to draw), but a new layout: a 2D wall you pan,
  not a vertical scroll. Needs care at phone size.
- **Risk:** least "journey"-like of the three. It feels like progress through a city, not a trip.

## 3. One long road, seen from the van

Keep a road, but make it a real road trip: a side-on (or three-quarter) drive through changing landscape,
like a scrolling diorama. Each level is a stop: a mailbox, a petrol station, a diner. You see the van
drive between them through the zone's weather and time of day (morning suburbs, noon city, sunset
canyon, night neon). Zones are separated by actual landmarks: a bridge, a tunnel, a mountain pass, each
with the toll gate in front of it. Detours are side roads that peel off and go somewhere (up the hill to
the ice cream stand). The Baron's billboards line the road and change as you beat him.

- **Why it fits:** it's the most "trip" of the three, and the most alive. Zones are unmistakable because
  the sky, light and landscape change. It also gives the residents and fake ads somewhere to be.
- **What it costs:** the most art: a side-view or diorama landscape per zone, parallax layers, a day/night
  cycle. Biggest build.
- **Risk:** levels become harder to scan (100 stops on a horizontal road); needs a zone overview to jump
  around.

## My pick

Swing 2 (the dispatch board) if the goal is the strongest identity for the least art, and it ties the
Baron story into the menu. Swing 3 if you want the trip feeling above everything and are happy to spend
on art. Swing 1 sits in between and is the most original.
